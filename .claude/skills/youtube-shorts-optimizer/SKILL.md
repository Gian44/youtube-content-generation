# YouTube Shorts Optimizer

Distilled from Dan the creator's "If your shorts get under 1,000 views... do this"
(creator with 75M+ views and 200K subscribers across two Shorts channels). Apply these rules
when generating Shorts scripts, metadata, schedules, or channel advice.

## The metric that decides everything: view-to-swipe ratio

The view-to-swipe ratio is the percentage of people who stop and watch the Short versus
swipe past it. It is the primary signal the Shorts algorithm uses to decide whether to keep
pushing a video.

- **Below 70%** — YouTube stops pushing the video. Most people are actively swiping away.
- **70%+** — the sweet spot; the video gets sustained distribution.
- **80%+** — near-guaranteed viral. (A 12M-view Short from the source creator ran at 86%.)

Every content rule below exists to push this number up. When making creative trade-offs,
choose the option that makes a stranger less likely to swipe in the first 2 seconds.

## Content rules (apply when creating any Short)

1. **Length: 20 seconds or less.** A viewer is far more likely to finish a 20-second video
   than a 40-second one, and completions drive the view-to-swipe ratio. Only exceed 20s when
   the content genuinely cannot be compressed — and never pad.
2. **Hook inside the first 2 seconds.** The opening must stop the scroll: eye-catching,
   intriguing, a curiosity gap or bold claim. If the first line could be deleted without
   losing the viewer, it's not a hook — rewrite it. Front-load the most surprising element.
3. **Title = curiosity + one natural keyword. Never hashtags in the title.** The title is
   prime real estate for making people want to watch; hashtags there are wasted space
   (MrBeast's Shorts contain zero title hashtags). Work a search keyword in only if it reads
   naturally.
4. **Exactly ~3 hashtags, hidden in the description.** This gives the SEO boost without
   polluting the title. More than 3-4 dilutes relevance.
5. **Tags: the 3-tier viral formula** — use 3-4 tags from each tier (9-12 total):

   | Tier | Purpose | How to write them |
   |------|---------|-------------------|
   | Post-specific | Tells the algorithm exactly what this video is | Describe the literal content (e.g. "bicep workout", "bicep curl", "best bicep exercise") |
   | Niche-specific | Targets the audience pool | The content category (e.g. "fitness", "bodybuilding", "health") |
   | Broad/viral | Reaches the general feed | "viral", "viral shorts", "shorts", "for you", "fyp" |

   Targeting the right audience matters: a Short shown to the wrong viewers gets swiped,
   which tanks the ratio and kills distribution.

## Output format for a generated Short

When asked to create a Short (script, concept, or metadata), ALWAYS deliver this block:

```
HOOK (0-2s):  [scroll-stopping opening line/visual]
BODY (2-17s): [content beats, tight, no filler]
PAYOFF (last ~3s): [resolution — ideally loops back to the hook]

TITLE: [curiosity-driven, natural keyword, NO hashtags]
TAGS:
  post-specific: [3-4 tags]
  niche:         [3-4 tags]
  broad:         [3-4: viral, viral shorts, shorts, for you]
DESCRIPTION: [1-2 lines + exactly 3 hashtags]
TARGET LENGTH: [seconds, <=20]
```

## Channel operations

- **Never post from a brand-new account.** YouTube suppresses fresh accounts that immediately
  start uploading (bot suspicion) and won't push their Shorts. Warm the account up for 2-3
  weeks minimum of normal human activity — watching, liking, commenting — before the first
  upload. (The source creator's main channels were ~3 months old before their first post.)
- **Upload exactly once per day** while under ~100K subscribers / pre-monetization. Daily
  uploads are essential for growth, but 2-3 per day is counterproductive: each Short needs
  time to sit and collect data, and the algorithm will starve a successful Short while it
  struggles to find an audience for a weak one posted the same day.
- **Be patient for 48 hours.** Shorts often idle (e.g. 6 views in 5 hours) and then spike
  past 10,000 after two days. Don't judge, delete, or change anything in the first 2 days.

## Rescue protocol: Short stuck under 1,000 views

When diagnosing a low-view Short, walk these causes in order and fix the first one that applies:

1. **View-to-swipe ratio under 70%?** → Hook/length problem. Cut to <=20s, rewrite the first
   2 seconds, lead with the strongest moment.
2. **Account brand new?** → Stop posting; warm the account up (see Channel operations).
3. **Established account, decent video, under ~100 views after 48 hours?** → It likely just
   missed the algorithm — the Shorts algorithm is temperamental and sometimes skips good
   videos entirely. Delete the video, change the title, and re-upload on a different day.
   (The source creator's 6M-view Short got 47 views in its first 48 hours; the re-upload
   went viral.)
4. **Tags missing or generic?** → Apply the 3-tier tags formula.
5. **Posting more than once a day?** → Drop to exactly one daily upload.
6. **Hashtags in the title?** → Remove them all; move 3 into the description.

If all six pass and views stay dead across multiple videos, the issue is content quality —
iterate on premise and hook, not metadata.

## Pre-publish checklist

Run before declaring any Short ready:

- [ ] Runtime <= 20 seconds
- [ ] Hook lands within the first 2 seconds
- [ ] Title is curiosity-driven, contains a natural keyword, zero hashtags
- [ ] 9-12 tags covering all 3 tiers
- [ ] Exactly ~3 hashtags in the description, none in the title
- [ ] No other upload scheduled on this channel today
- [ ] Account is warmed up / established
